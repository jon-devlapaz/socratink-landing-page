# Domain patterns

Use these adaptations after completing the common diagnostic. Do not let the domain shortcut the evidence ledger or ethics gate.

## Contents

1. Physical products
2. Digital products
3. Services
4. Offers
5. Brands
6. Customer experiences

## 1. Physical products

Inspect performance, durability, repairability, safety, materials, packaging, sensory feedback, shelf or delivery context, and after-sale support. Test whether a quality signal improves accurate quality inference rather than merely luxury association. Include shipping, waste, and accessibility costs.

## 2. Digital products

Inspect time to first value, reliability, interaction clarity, defaults, privacy, integrations, support, pricing metric, and switching cost. Measure direct customer effort—time, steps, errors, or reported difficulty—separately from activation, conversion, and retention. Avoid decorative “premium” treatments that reduce usability or performance. Never manufacture scarcity for infinitely reproducible capacity; use real support, cohort, compute, or access constraints only when enforceable.

## 3. Services

Inspect outcome consistency, expertise, process transparency, responsiveness, handoffs, wait time, emotional effort, recovery, and proof from comparable clients. For proof assets, name the required consent, confidentiality/privacy, legal/compliance, and delivery-verification owners; measure buyer trust separately from lead progression. A named process can improve comprehension, but it must describe a real method. Capacity limits must reflect actual delivery constraints.

## 4. Offers

Inspect the core deliverable, audience fit, outcome, proof, time to result, customer effort, risk allocation, total cost, payment terms, guarantees, and real reason to act now. Improve the underlying offer before adding bonuses or urgency. Avoid inflated “value stacks.”

## 5. Brands

Inspect whether identity, language, visual systems, provenance, public behavior, policies, and actual delivery tell the same story. Distinctiveness without relevance is decoration; aspiration without proof is posturing. Measure recognition, accurate association, trust, and fit separately.

## 6. Customer experiences

Map the end-to-end journey, including discovery, purchase, onboarding, use, support, renewal, cancellation, and recovery. Find the worst contradiction and the first meaningful win. Design peaks and endings only after removing material failure and friction. Include employees and operators when service conditions affect delivery.
