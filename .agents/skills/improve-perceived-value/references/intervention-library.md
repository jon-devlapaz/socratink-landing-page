# Intervention library

Use this library only after identifying the binding gap. These are mechanisms to test, not universal tactics. Read the relevant family rather than loading the entire catalogue by default.

Consult [mechanism-evidence.md](mechanism-evidence.md) before assigning an evidence grade to anchoring, decoys, scarcity, social norms, self-assembly, fluency, sensory cues, choice architecture, or peak–end experience design.

## 1. Improve delivered value

Addresses delivery and fit gaps.

- Remove a recurring defect or service inconsistency.
- Improve the core outcome before adding features.
- Narrow the audience or use case to one the subject serves well.
- Reduce time to first useful result.
- Remove setup, learning, decision, or coordination work.
- Improve accessibility, safety, reliability, repairability, or support.
- Make total cost lower or more predictable.
- Create a genuinely better tier, service level, material, or method.

Evidence should come primarily from product, service, customer, and operational data—not persuasion research.

## 2. Make outcomes legible

Addresses translation and salience gaps.

- Translate features into audience-specific outcomes and tradeoffs.
- Use concrete demonstrations, samples, previews, or before/after states.
- Name a real method or process so it can be understood and evaluated.
- Explain provenance, materials, craftsmanship, specifications, or operating standards when they matter to quality.
- Use plain-language comparisons against the actual next-best alternative.
- Connect the immediate outcome to the audience’s broader job without exaggerating it.
- Make important limitations visible; bounded claims often increase trust.

Avoid scientific-sounding language, foreign naming, or technical detail when they add theater rather than useful information.

## 3. Increase warranted confidence

Addresses evidence and credibility gaps.

- Show comparable customer outcomes with consent and context.
- Provide auditable counts, reviews, certifications, test results, or service records.
- Use demonstrations or trials that let customers experience the claim.
- Match proof to the audience segment and failure concern.
- Explain how the method handles likely failure paths.
- Offer a proportionate, enforceable guarantee or reversible adoption path.
- Use legitimate expert review, press, awards, or customer logos only when relevant and authorized.

More proof is not always better. Prefer a few diagnostic proof points over a wall of weak logos.

## 4. Reduce effort, delay, and risk

Addresses delivery, friction, and credibility gaps.

- Engineer a faster first win.
- Simplify onboarding, setup, packaging, navigation, or service handoffs.
- Provide defaults, templates, migration help, or done-for-you options.
- Reduce choice count or recommend a fit-based default.
- Split payment or commitment only when total cost remains transparent.
- Offer samples, trials, pilots, or staged commitments with clear exit terms.
- Surface support, maintenance, cancellation, and recovery paths.

Do not use friction asymmetrically—easy entry and hard exit is a dark pattern.

## 5. Improve coherent quality signals

Addresses signal gaps when actual quality is already present.

- Align material, weight, finish, sound, motion, scent, temperature, or tactile feedback with real product quality and context.
- Improve visual hierarchy, typography, spacing, imagery, paper, packaging, or environmental details.
- Create a distinctive but usable shape, silhouette, interaction, or service ritual.
- Make sustainable materials and sourcing verifiable.
- Ensure premium signals do not reduce accessibility, durability, usability, or repairability.
- Make the unboxing, opening, arrival, or handoff sequence coherent with the promised experience.

Most claims in this family are context-sensitive. Default to `promising` or `anecdotal` until direct testing supports them.

## 6. Structure comparison and choice

Addresses salience and decision-friction gaps.

- Use clear tiers tied to distinct audiences or levels of delivered value.
- Choose a pricing metric that scales with customer value.
- Frame monthly, annual, per-use, or total cost transparently for the actual decision.
- Bundle components that solve one job together.
- Show a truthful reference point, alternative, or total-cost comparison.
- Highlight a recommended option based on fit, not seller preference alone.
- Use a premium tier to reveal differentiated value only when it is a legitimate option.

Avoid dominated decoys, fake list prices, hidden fees, or preselected paid choices that compromise informed consent.

## 7. Strengthen social and identity fit

Addresses fit, credibility, and memory gaps.

- Show credible use by people or organizations similar to the audience.
- Curate authorized customer-created examples that reveal real use.
- Make community size or participation visible only with auditable counts.
- Express a founder, origin, or craft story that explains real decisions.
- Use language and imagery that help the intended audience recognize itself without stereotyping or exclusion.
- Create legitimate membership, qualification, geographic, cohort, or capacity boundaries.

Never fabricate popularity, exclusivity, endorsements, or “people viewing now” signals.

## 8. Improve experience and memory

Addresses experienced-value, trust-contradiction, and memory gaps.

- Remove the worst moment in the journey before embellishing the best.
- Create an early success and a clear, satisfying completion.
- Align support, environment, packaging, communication, and policies with the promise.
- Add useful progress feedback, acknowledgement, or closure.
- Make service recovery visible and effective.
- Design a distinctive ritual or interaction only when it serves the job.
- Ask customers what they remember later; do not assume designed moments are remembered.

The strongest experience signal is consistent delivery across touchpoints.
